Can a learned ranking help teams choose which content to review first?

A leakage-aware comparison of a transparent content-refresh rule and Logistic Regression, with a human-review action playbook.
Chaman Afzaal · FlyRank ML Internship · 29 October 2026

01 · Abstract

Question, method, result, and use

FlyRank helps teams create and optimize website content, but search performance can quietly weaken across thousands of pages while people have limited time to review them. To test whether a learned ranking could help decide what to inspect first, I used FlyRank’s anonymized search-performance resources and benchmarked Logistic Regression on a separate 30,000-row starter slice, while documenting the 78,835,655-row warehouse release as context rather than model-training data. On a client-held-out test set of 7,115 starter-slice rows across eight unseen clients, Logistic Regression achieved average precision 0.529, Precision@20 0.600, and Precision@50 0.640, compared with 0.504, 0.450, and 0.440 for the transparent Week-4 freshness-and-visibility rule. The test set’s observed decline-label rate was 0.517, with zero client overlap between training and test data. This measured result supports further human-reviewed testing of a ranking aid; it does not show that refreshing a page causes recovery or that these starter-slice metrics generalize to a model trained on the full warehouse.

02 · Introduction / problem statement

Limited time means review order matters

FlyRank’s content workflow connects research, publishing, and search-performance monitoring. In that workflow, content can quietly lose search visibility while a team is busy creating new pages or managing a large existing library. With thousands of items and limited review time, the operational problem is concrete: which page should a person inspect first, and what evidence explains that priority?

The product already uses hand-written signals such as health scores, quick-win tags, and needs-attention flags to surface possible actions. Those rules are useful and understandable, but fixed thresholds may not capture every combination of content age, content characteristics, and keyword context. This capstone tests whether Logistic Regression can rank items carrying an observed decline label more effectively than one transparent freshness-and-visibility rule. Existing product flags and target-derived trend fields are excluded from model features to avoid circular evaluation.

The goal is not to replace a content strategist or automatically rewrite a page. It is to provide a second, evidence-based review order that a person can inspect, question, and override. Because the benchmark label is an observed trend proxy rather than a measured refresh outcome, the results are framed as decision-support evidence—not proof that any recommended edit will recover traffic.

What is excluded and why

  • Client names, domains, page URLs, raw search queries, query hashes, credentials, row-level outputs, and data exports are not published.
  • content_id and client_id are used only for grouping and joins, never as model features.
  • The label, trend_direction, trend_pct, direct 30-day trend inputs, current traffic totals, and product-generated flags are excluded from the model features.
  • The final-month warehouse sample was not used to design label logic. The full-release prototype’s outcome-window-overlapping features are not used as headline evidence.

The warehouse includes a mix of GSC and GA4 availability. The data guide warns that history depth differs by client and unavailable GA4 values may be zero-filled with an explicit availability flag. These differences limit simple cross-client comparisons.

04 · Methodology

Target, features, baseline, and validation

Target

The starter benchmark defines is_declining_label = (trend_direction == "down"). This is an observed trend label from the snapshot—not a verified future-period forecast and not a label for successful recovery after a refresh.

Model features

Logistic Regression uses content_age_days, days_since_last_update, word_count, char_count, search_volume, competition, cpc, content_type, main_intent, and competition_level. Numeric missing values are median-imputed with missingness indicators and scaled. Categorical missing values are labeled “unknown” and one-hot encoded. Preprocessing is fitted only on training rows.

Baseline and validation

The Week-4 baseline selects pages at least 180 days since their last update and with at least 500 impressions over 90 days, ranking qualifying pages by impressions. The benchmark uses a fixed GroupShuffleSplit with test_size=0.25 and seed 42, grouping by client. The saved run has 22,885 training rows from 24 clients and 7,115 test rows from eight clients; client overlap is zero. Both rankings are evaluated on the same test rows.

Leakage checks

The Week-6 audit reports passing checks for label exclusion, trend-field exclusion, IDs, direct 30-day inputs, current traffic totals/product flags, and client overlap. The audit also notes the snapshot cannot prove every remaining feature was captured strictly before a future outcome window. A separate warehouse prototype used last-30-day position volatility and fixed-window query signals that may overlap its label period; those results are not treated as validated headline evidence here.

06 · Limitations & honest framing

What this work cannot claim

  • The headline benchmark uses the 30,000-row starter snapshot. It is not a model trained on all 78.8 million daily warehouse rows.
  • The label is an observed trend proxy, not a future forecast and not a refresh-success outcome.
  • Client grouping reduces client leakage, but the validation is not time-forward. Seasonality, measurement changes, and future distribution shift remain possible.
  • The baseline and model use different information, so this is an operational comparison rather than an equal-feature experiment.
  • The 181+ day bucket contains only 174 rows. Its rate is not a stable population estimate.
  • A static snapshot cannot show that updating a page causes performance to improve. Page selection can be influenced by factors absent from the data.
  • Warehouse history and analytics availability vary by client. The initial warehouse model prototype had outcome-window overlap in some features and is not used as validated headline evidence.
Safe conclusion: On this held-out starter-slice test, Logistic Regression ranked the observed decline label better than the Week-4 rule at the top 20 and top 50. This is directional decision-support evidence for further time-aware testing; it does not establish future decline or causal impact.
07 · Ranked recommendations

Human-review action playbook

The queue is a review list, not an automatic refresh instruction. It uses only freshness and impressions, not the observed decline label, trend fields, or model predictions.

  1. Stale and visible — review first. At least 180 days since update and at least 500 impressions in 90 days. Check intent, accuracy, freshness, and competing pages before deciding whether to edit. Reason code: stale_visible_review.
  2. Visible but recently updated — inspect the search result. At least 500 impressions but less than 180 days since update. Review title/snippet alignment, intent, and possible cannibalization. Reason code: visible_recent_review.
  3. Stale with low visibility — diagnose first. At least 180 days since update but fewer than 500 impressions. Check usefulness and possible explanations for low visibility. Reason code: stale_low_visibility_check.
  4. Missing signal — verify data quality. Do not assign an action until freshness and impression fields are checked. Reason code: missing_signal_manual_check.
  5. Routine monitoring. Do not spend refresh effort based on this queue alone. Reason code: routine_monitor.

Every item needs human review. The rule is a transparent way to allocate attention; it is not proof that a page is declining or that a refresh will improve outcomes.
